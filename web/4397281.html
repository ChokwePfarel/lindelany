What Do We See First?

Salience, Framing & The Architecture of Attention

The Netflix interface is not neutral. Its homepage layout, the top category bar, the download and search buttons, the centred recommendation,all guide user attention before you've consciously made a choice.

Through layout, positioning, and visual hierarchy, certain South African shows are made more visible than others. Large thumbnails, bold white fonts, and strategic placement shape what appears important.

"Fatal Seduction" is immediately highly salient, its intimate visual composition and dramatic character positioning communicate tone and genre at a glance. "Blood & Water" is comparatively less salient, offering fewer immediate narrative cues.

Salience refers to the prominence given to specific issues or stories, making them stand out in the public eye. Netflix uses a white font for almost every show title, a standardising technique that applies equally to local and global content.

Which Stories Are Told?

Netflix's South African Catalogue & The Politics of Representation

Netflix's South African catalogue often highlights urban, dramatic, and globally recognisable narratives , a polished and marketable version of South African life that aligns with global entertainment expectations.

Blood & Water is a teen crime drama set in Cape Town, following Puleng Khumalo as she investigates whether a swimming star at an elite school is her abducted sister. It explores privilege, secrets, and romance in a setting visually legible to international audiences.

Fatal Seduction revolves around Nandi, a married professor whose passionate affair with a younger man spirals into betrayal, lies, and murder. Its thriller genre conventions are broadly universal, reducing the friction of cultural translation.

"While these shows may appear authentic, they prioritise experiences that are easily understood by international audiences." , From Mzansi to The World, Critical Analysis

Although Netflix includes familiar South African actors and settings, these representations remain selective. Certain identities are amplified because they are more marketable, while others remain less visible.

From a Critical Discourse Analysis (CDA) perspective, these choices reflect broader power relations within global media systems. Netflix does not simply reflect South African reality , it shapes and filters it in ways that align with global consumption.

Critical Questions Raised

  • Whose stories are being told, and whose are excluded?
  • What version of South Africa does global Netflix present?
  • How does marketability determine cultural visibility?
  • Who holds the power to define "South African" storytelling?

Who Gets Heard?

Multilingualism, Subtitles & The Mediation of Local Voices

South Africa is a multilingual society with 11 official languages, yet English dominates Netflix productions. While other languages appear in dialogue, they are frequently accompanied by subtitles , positioning them as secondary, as something requiring translation.

The Netflix playback interface offers subtitle and audio options in numerous global languages. Yet South African languages are often absent as subtitle choices, raising a pressing question: why does the platform prioritise global languages over local ones?

In a scene from Blood & Water shared on the Netflix South Africa TikTok page, a character speaks isiXhosa. Subtitles provide a direct translation, making the dialogue accessible to a broader audience.

While this can be read as a form of inclusivity, it simultaneously positions isiXhosa as something that requires interpretation, rather than centring it as a primary mode of communication. The language is present but mediated , visible yet framed as foreign.

This connects to the politics of presence (Phillips, 1995), which examines how certain identities are made visible while others are marginalised within public systems.

Politics of Presence:
What Is Really Happening?

How identity becomes a product , curated, packaged, and made globally consumable.

🎭

Social Semiotics

Meaning is constructed through the interaction of images, layout, language, and sound. Every design choice on the Netflix platform is a meaning-making act that shapes how South Africa is perceived.

⚖️

Critical Discourse Analysis

These representations are shaped by power and global media structures. Netflix does not reflect South African reality , it filters it through the lens of global marketability and commercial viability.

👁️

Politics of Presence

Phillips (1995) shows how visibility is structured by systems of inclusion and exclusion. Certain cultures, languages, and lifestyles are made visible because they are marketable; others remain marginalised.
